“Close,
but No Cookie”

How do we understand a loss? Data? Emotion? Systemic failure?

India’s dominance in the World Cup cannot be understated.

This team had not lost — and that’s fact. But they lost the big game, and as of late that had become a common theme. Since 2011 we had not won any World Cup.

It seemed like it was this team, this squad of heroes for so many in this country, set to achieve a long-awaited glory — a promised final feather in the cap for a lot of that senior team. But “one bad day” can stop even the invincible in sport.

Inevitable India

The feeling around the cricketing world

The sentiment that India would win in its own backyard. Luck? You can be lucky. Outplayed? A little. But in the end — we lost that day.

India winning the 2023 World Cup felt inevitable — a given and an afterthought that had already happened. And yet now the thing we have left is a sour taste.

On the road to the final, we had won all our games, dominating our opponents. India on the pitch were relentless, hunting their targets on the field and on the scoreboard — our bowling averaging all-time lows and all our batters averaging all-time highs. We did learn that average does mean all things even out. In the final we faced an opponent that refused to lie down and refused to let us have our way even for a minute. In a stadium bathed in blue, the small portion of yellow is what deafened us all.

Australia, again

From Ponting to Head

Australia pulled off one of the — if not the — biggest upsets in cricket, and this is not even the first time they have done this to India.

From Ponting in 2003 to Travis Head in 2023, India faced a team that just refused to lose on the big stage. They want it — they want it as bad as they breathe — and so did we. We just got left breathless.

2003 Johannesburg Ricky Ponting, 140* Australia won by 125 runs
2023 Ahmedabad Travis Head, 137 Australia won by 6 wickets

Two World Cup finals, twenty years apart. Same opponent, same result.

Let’s deconstruct a hard day to remember in ICC history and look at what the stats tell us.

                    The
                    Final

                    Let’s now get to the inevitable day. The city was bathed in blue shirts and a sense of festivity — an occasion had come upon the country. Every TV, cellphone and radio station that day was dedicated to this game. The players were ready, the crowd was in, the day was set and the toss was ready.

                    The captains stood apart, looking, glancing and calling. The call went to Patrick James Cummins, who put India in to bat first. By most pundits and discourse, that was the wrong choice. Australia were counting on the dew to come into play, and maybe they took a gambler’s chance — they still played the better cricket game on the day.

                    2BattingA batter short

                    In the end it was our star-studded and rampant batting that let us down — a lineup that averaged more than 50 with all main batters. On the day we had a batter less: losing Hardik Pandya was one of the biggest blows to India’s balance as a team. We had to play a batter short, and in turn the other batters placed a higher value on their wicket.

                      Holding your nerve

                      After all our effort, after our campaign into the World Cup, we lost the “big” game.

                      In professional sport, everyone playing plays at such a high level that the only thing that matters is: can you do what is needed? And when needed, can you do what is beyond your need, for the team’s sake?

                      A simple culture difference.

                      In India, when a player comes home after a match, the first question is alwaysHow did you do?
                      In Australia, the first question is alwaysDid your team win?

                      On the day the better team won, and this is how it is in sport.

                      After the final, one thing I do distinctly remember is just how quiet Bangalore was. Even the busier parts of town sounded like everyone was grieving in silence.

                      Grief must be overcome now.

                      Already?

                      As we look ahead to next year — another World Cup. Already?

                      But this could be a bitter one. A lot of our stalwarts are coming to play their last tournament, and for those who played in 2023, this comes as a point for them to prove.

                      The new Indian team looks starved. They want to win, but only time will tell who can win in that place on that day — because, as we’ve seen,

                      nothing is written in stone.
